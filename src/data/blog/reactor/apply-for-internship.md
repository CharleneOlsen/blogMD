As per requirements of the previous semester I had to apply for an internship that would last at least three weeks. At first, I was terrified, not just because it's a new place, new people, and just overall too many things could go wrong. But mainly because I do computational chemistry. I create simulations, and I'm more a data scientist than a girl in a laboratory. So, the idea of me being in a professional laboratory that does a high-tech research terrified me quite a lot. But when I look back at the whole internship, I see how great of an experience it really was and how much I've grown and learnt from it. 

Let's kick this off with how I found this opportunity. I, at first, wanted to do something regarding computational chemistry, because that is what I'm best at. But a lot of companies that work in this area either don't do internships at all or just couldn't provide me a work for only three weeks, which is totally fair. But my deadline was coming fast, and my anxiety grew with it. 

Then the school's career fair happened and with it came an opportunity to talk directly with people from HR, who for sure will have more information that corporate websites. Fortunately, I was able to find one very exciting opportunity in **MemBrain**. This company focuses on researching and manufacturing membranes for many different usages, such as osmosis and electrodialysis. Not a topic I'm super familiar with, but I gave it a shot and applied for internship at their company anyway. And oh my, this was one of the best decision I have ever made. People at the company were incredibly nice and answered my email right away, and thus my adventure began.

What was great about this internship, is that they provided a living arrangement as well as lunches and on top of that I got paid. This is a dream internship really. I traveled to place near Liberec in the Czech Republic called Stráž pod Ralskem and I lived there in a very spacious room for three weeks.

## What I did there?
At first, I had to sign all the corporate documents, as well as a NDA, so I can't really talk a lot about the particular topic, because it's a research-stage technology. 

I worked in a lab, where technicians carry out their work. My job was carrying out batch experiments to test selectivity of membranes I was provided with. The work itself was more monotonous, because I had to at first prepare batch solutions which I would feed into my electrodialysis unit and whilst the experiment was running I was taking samples to then test them for the amount of ions. From this data I then was able to determine the selectivity of membranes. Of course, I was changing initial conditions to see how selectivity would change if I would apply different current, different solution ration concentration and different volumetric flow. 

What I had performed was a true engineering work, I was changing variables to better understand how selectivity of the membrane changes depending on different conditions. This is very common in any engineering work. You are very much interested in how your reactor, or overall your unit, technology behaves under different circumstances because only then you can understand it better. You find critical points this way, you find maximum temperature, but also optimal conditions which are ideal conditions when your technology outputs the best product, or biggest amount. 

## Who should apply for an internship?
You may think, as I did, that if you want to go into research, you don't really need to do a lot of internships. Wrong. You definitely should. Maybe not because of scouting for a potential job, but for broadening your horizons, to go out of your cocoon and experience the world outside your little cozy lab. Also, it's a great way how to get a sneak peek into a different topic. Even though I only dipped my toes into membrane technology, it helped me tremendously in understanding not only membranes as a whole but all the different aspects of chemical engineering. 

So, if you are on the fence if you should do it or not, definitely do. Because it will give you confidence. It gave me a confidence in what I do. It helped me to believe in my own conclusions, in my own decisions. But I really have to thank the MemBrain team for this opportunity, because without their support and very welcoming atmosphere I wouldn't feel as comfortable in making decisions by myself.
