## My thoughts about the book
It is very interesting to see the world of drag queens through the eyes of the most iconic drag queen there is. RuPaul talks about his identity, and I just understood, what was so fascinating and appealing for me on them. Their identity. He writes: "Drag says, "Identity is a joke"". I always admired about drag queens their ability to have fun. To dress for a feeling and situation. They play with social norms and gender and I truly love it. Because to be able to not take seriously your own appearances and gender, it takes a lot of courage. And I believe it is due to the peoples' need to pigeonhole everything and everyone. We need to categorise everything. We put these labels, stereotypes on every group there is, and we just love to give ourselves identity in the society to feel like we belong. It is only natural, but it is so funny that these people then criticizes LGBTQ+ communities for labeling themselves, even thought they do it their whole life. Oh, I have a wife and kids, I have a son, I drive this car, I have a house, I am a member of this political group, I live here, and so on. But when somebody identifies as a trans, or gay, or queer, or anything their heart desires, oh, they are a snowflake, or they are mentally ill. It is sad to live in this world sometimes unfortunately and people are very weird. 

I wholeheartedly recommend this book to anyone who wants a sneak peek to the world of drag. RuPaul is one of the best examples of what it is to be a drag queen and this collection of tweets and quotes helped me better understand this beautiful and sparkly world. 

>If you can’t love yourself, how in the hell are you gonna love somebody else? Can I get an amen?

## Observation
I feel that him trying to find his signature look is so spot on as an allegory to life. He didn't find his look straight away, but through a lot of thinking, inspiration from artists and people around him, he was able to experiment with different looks until something felt natural. He is very inspirational human being, because he is truly free. It doesn't mean everybody should be a drag queen, but I do feel that today's society had lost a sense of creativity and experimenting. I see that a lot in girlhood today. Do you remember yourself as a kid? I used to wear mismatching clothing, also because we were poor, but I experimented a lot with what I had. I used to steal mom's make-up and do horrible work with eyeliner which made me look like I'm from a horror movie, and I still wore that to school. I do feel that it is also in large because we didn't have a dress code in school but also because there was no social media, so nobody was monitoring you through camera about the way you look and way you dress. This gave me also freedom to experiment with who I am, what clothing is comfortable to me and what kind of look I'm going for.
